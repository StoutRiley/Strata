SMART goals

A checklist for writing goals and steps clearly. It was introduced by George T. Doran in 1981. A goal or step is SMART when it is:

Specific: it says exactly what you will do. "Book 3 paid vocal gigs" beats "get more gigs."
Measurable: you can tell when it is done, with a number or a clear yes or no.
Achievable: it stretches you but is realistic given your time, skills, and resources.
Relevant: it clearly moves you toward a goal that matters to you.
Time-bound: it has a target date. Every step here has a date field for this.

In this app: write each step's title and description so another person could tell whether it is finished.

OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

A way to set ambitious goals and track progress. It was developed at Intel by Andy Grove and popularized at Google by John Doerr.

Objective: what you want to achieve, stated in a short, motivating sentence. Example: "Become a go-to local vocalist."
Key results: usually 2 to 5 measurable outcomes that show the objective is met. Example: "Perform 12 paid shows" and "Grow my mailing list to 500." They measure results, not tasks.
Scoring: teams often score each key result from 0 to 1 (or 0 to 100%) at the end of a cycle, often quarterly. Many treat about 0.7 as a strong result for a stretch target.

In this app: use the goal title as the objective, write the key results in the goal description, and use steps for the work that gets you there.

Signal-to-noise ratio

The idea comes from engineering and communications. The signal is the useful information. The noise is everything that hides it. For goals, the signal is the few actions and measurements that really move you forward, and the noise is busywork, distractions, and vanity numbers.

Example: the number of paid bookings each week is signal. The number of likes on a post is noise unless it leads to bookings.

A higher ratio means more of your time goes to what matters. To raise it, cut steps that do not serve the goal, track only a few measures, and put the most important work first.

In this app: the Priorities page shows only the next few steps from each goal. Delete steps and tasks that do not earn their place.
